## 05 — What Acon does (two column)

**Kicker:** 01 — The product

**Headline:** Describe a building, or hand it a drawing set.

**Body:** Acon is an assistant that actually produces the model. Tell it what
you want in plain words, or give it the drawings for a project, and it builds a
complete 3D building: floors, rooms, walls, doors and windows, and the services

## 08 — Why nothing else does this (4-up)

**Kicker:** 02 — The difference

**Headline:** Why nothing else does this.

**Design software** — Powerful, but somebody still has to draw the entire
building. The AI features that exist run as online services.

**Early-stage layout tools** — Useful for quick studies of what fits on a site.
They won't read an issued drawing set, and they run in the cloud.

**AI chat products** — They will happily describe a building. They can't hand
you one you can measure, cost, or edit.

**Drawing-to-model services** — Accurate, but the files are sent away to another
company and the result comes back days later.

**Footer:** The gap: a building produced automatically, from our own documents,
on our own equipment.

---

## 09 — The difference (two column)

**Kicker:** 02 — The difference

**Headline:** It doesn't draw the building. It decides what the building is.

**Body:** This is the part that makes everything else work. Other tools try to
generate the shape itself, and what comes back is a picture or a lump of
geometry — nothing anyone can build from. Acon splits the job in two. The model
decides what the building should be: how big, how many floors, which rooms sit
where. The software then constructs it precisely from those decisions. That is
why the result behaves like a building instead of a render.

**Right column — Why that matters**
- Every dimension is a real dimension
- Nothing is guessed into place
- Same result from words, drawings, or an address
- The model can be swapped without breaking it
- The building stays editable afterwards

## 10 — What only Acon produces

**Kicker:** 02 — The difference

**Headline:** What comes out that no other tool produces.

- **Services that are properly coordinated** — pipes, ducts, power and data each
  keep their own space in the ceiling and their own line down through the
  building, the way a real set of drawings resolves them.
- **Drawings, not just a picture** — floor levels, roof heights, dimensions and a
  title block, in the language a construction set already uses.
- **Buildings that behave like buildings** — upper floors step back correctly,
  towers taper, and a spire is added when only its tip height is known.
- **Measured facts kept apart from generated ones** — when a building is looked
  up by address, the real outline is labelled as measured, so nobody mistakes it
  for a suggestion.
- **Changes by conversation** — resize a room, take the roof off, change a
  finish. The building updates; nothing is rebuilt from scratch.
- **The build, played back** — site, slab, structure, walls, roof and services
  appear in order, so you can watch how it goes together.

**Footer:** All of it produced automatically, and all of it able to run in-house.

## 12 — Why not the cloud (3-up)

**Kicker:** 03 — The reason

**Headline:** Three reasons this cannot live in the cloud.

**The drawings are confidential** — Blueprints, structural drawings and tender
documents belong to clients. Many agreements simply do not allow them to be
handed to another company for processing.

**Today the thinking happens elsewhere** — The part of Acon that interprets a
project currently runs on an outside service, so every request sends project
information out of the office.

**Modelling by hand is the bottleneck** — Turning a received drawing set into a
usable 3D model takes days of skilled time, and it starts again with every
revision.

---

## 13 — Inside the firm

**Kicker:** 03 — The reason

**Headline:** The software has to live inside the firm.

- Nothing leaves the office. The drawings stay exactly where they already are.
- Nothing to declare to a client, because no outside company ever handles their
  documents.
- No question of what happens to the files afterwards. They are never sent
  anywhere, so there is no policy to take on trust.
- It keeps working without an internet connection — on site, on secure floors,
  anywhere.
- No subscription per person and no charge per revision. The cost is the
  machine, once.

## 16 — The machine (4-up)

**Kicker:** 04 — The machine

**Headline:** A supercomputer that fits on a desk.

**Purpose-built** — Designed by Dell and NVIDIA for running AI models on your
own premises, rather than an ordinary workstation pressed into service.

**Room for large models** — 128 GB of memory, shared between processor and
graphics. That is what lets a genuinely capable model sit on the machine at once.

**Serious speed** — Up to a thousand trillion calculations a second, so a
request comes back while someone waits, not minutes later.

**Room to grow** — Handles models far larger than we need today. Two machines
side by side handle twice as much again.

**Footer:** It sits under a desk, runs on ordinary office power, and the same
setup moves to larger hardware later if it is ever needed.

---

## 17 — The models (three column)

**Kicker:** 05 — The models

**Headline:** The model has to be able to read a drawing.

**It reads the sheets** — Qwen-VL is a model that sees as well as reads. It
looks at a drawing the way a person does — finding the dimensions, the room
names, the levels, and where each of them sits on the page.

**It plans the building** — The same model then decides what the building is,
and hands those decisions to the software to construct. One model covers both
jobs, so there is less to look after.

**Sized to the machine** — These models are openly available and free to run, in
sizes made for hardware like this. We start with a smaller one to prove it works
and move up only if accuracy asks for it.

**Footer:** Everything runs on the machine in the office. The outside service in
use today is switched off.

## 18 — How it works (steps)

**Kicker:** 05 — How it works

**Headline:** From documents to a building, in five steps.

**01 Hand it over** — Drawings, a scan, or a photograph of a marked-up sheet.

**02 Read** — The model on the office machine reads the sheet: sizes, levels,
room names.

**03 Decide** — It works out what the building is: outline, floors, rooms,
heights.

**04 Build** — The software constructs the model precisely from those decisions.

**05 Use it** — Walk through it, measure it, and change it by asking.

## 23 — Being realistic (3-up)

**Kicker:** 06 — Being realistic

**Headline:** What to be realistic about.

**Fast, not infinite** — One machine comfortably serves a team, not the whole
firm at once. If use grows, we add a second.

**A specialist machine** — It is set up for AI work rather than everyday office
software, so installing it properly takes a little time.

**Reading drawings needs checking** — Messy scans and handwritten dimensions are
where these models make mistakes. We test against drawings with known answers
before anyone relies on it.

## 25 — The plan (roadmap)

**Kicker:** 06 — The plan

**Headline:** Prove it, then rely on it.

**Now · Set up** — The machine in the office, the model running on it, and one

demand grows. Nothing needs rewriting.
